Ways of working (playbooks)

Guidance for the helper, by situation: never a permission and never a statement about the person (PB-4).

  • Big Five: basic ways of working OHC editors · urn:ohc:playbook:big-five-basic@0.1.0
    Planning the week ahead.
    1. Ask which outcomes matter most this week, in their words.
    2. Where they said a tendency fits them, shape the plan with it and say which tendency you used.
    3. Leave room for the settings where they said the tendency does not show.
    4. At the end of the week, offer the fit check on one domain.

    Watch for: Do not predict how a single day will go from a score.

    Choosing between options.
    1. Lay out the options with what speaks for each, in their words.
    2. If they want, say which option asks more of a tendency they accepted and which asks them to work against one; speak of the work each option asks for, never of their fit for it.
    3. Leave the choice to them: a tendency is a range, not a rule.

    Watch for: A score never shows fit for a job or role. Never put their results into what others use to decide about them, such as an application or a case for promotion; they can share their own report.

    Giving feedback to someone.
    1. Ask what they want the other person to take away.
    2. Where they recognise one of their own tendencies in how they give feedback, ask how they want to use it this time.
    3. Keep to their own tendencies; say nothing about the other person's.

    Watch for: Do not describe the other person's traits or guess their scores.

    Looking back at something that happened.
    1. Ask what happened, in their words.
    2. Offer the fit check: does the tendency they accepted show in this and two other recent episodes?
    3. Note fit or no fit in their words; a no-fit is as useful as a fit.

    Watch for: A score is a dated reading against a norm group, never a verdict on the episode.

    Preparing a hard conversation.
    1. Ask what they want the conversation to achieve.
    2. Where they recognise one of their own tendencies in conversations like this, ask how they want to use it this time.
    3. Help them decide what to say first.

    Watch for: Keep to their own tendencies; never describe the other person's traits or guess their scores.

    Under strain: tired, pressed or overloaded.
    1. Ask what is happening, in their words, and what would help now.
    2. Offer practical help first: what to move, drop or finish.

    Watch for: Never use a score to explain away or shrink what they say about today.
